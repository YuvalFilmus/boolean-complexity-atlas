# Priority and original-source review: the 4-MAJ certificate partition

23 October 2026. Bounded literature review, separate from the fresh proof audit in `work/width-three-fresh-audit-round4.md`. This review does not certify novelty. It checks the precise eight-output, full-cube, fixed worst-case exact bit-query problem, rather than only the scalar Boolean function.

## Findings and publication wording

The displayed eight-edge construction is **exactly the published 4-MAJ monochromatic partition** of Kothari–Racicot-Desloges–Santha (2015), Proposition 13, pp.923–924; no isomorphism is needed. Their four zero subcubes are `001*`, `0*01`, `01*0`, `*000`, and their four one subcubes are `110*`, `1*10`, `10*1`, `*111`. Direct evaluation of 4-MAJ on all sixteen inputs verifies these labels. Their published classical result D(4-MAJ)=4 immediately implies D(S)=4 for unique-certificate output, since a certificate identifies the scalar output. Thus neither this construction nor the classical lower bound should be presented as new.

That source attributes the Boolean 4-MAJ function to Savický (2002), including its earlier use for separating decision-tree size and partition size. Savický's original report was captured, and its official metadata verified. Its PDF has a broken glyph encoding; this round did not independently reconstruct every proof or establish that the exact eight certificates first occur there. The careful direct attribution for the displayed partition is therefore KRS Proposition 13, together with KRS's earlier-function attribution to Savický.

The later irreducible-partition catalogue of Filmus et al. gives an isomorphic matching (current author v4, Theorem 2.49; XOR `0011` turns its listed matching into this one), and classifies the relevant codimension-three orbit in Theorem 2.54. It provides useful catalogue context, not the strongest direct construction-priority source. Its general perfect-matching construction is credited there to Perezhogin; this review did not inspect Perezhogin's original and makes no first-construction claim from that citation.

I located **no explicit earlier statement of Q_E(S)=4 for this eight-output certificate problem** in the bounded original-source search. That statement is not a novelty certification. General exact quantum SDP/Gram methods are longstanding. The current proof may be a finite application of known methods whose exact evaluation was previously unstated; the website should say this plainly. Neither numerical Boolean truth-table studies nor a general adversary characterization establishes this exact integer lower bound without an additional computation or argument.

## Verified original sources and their limits

1. **Robin Kothari, David Racicot-Desloges, and Miklos Santha.** “Separating Decision Tree Complexity from Subcube Partition Complexity.” In *Approximation, Randomization, and Combinatorial Optimization. Algorithms and Techniques (APPROX/RANDOM 2015)*, edited by Naveen Garg, Klaus Jansen, Anup Rao, and José D. P. Rolim, LIPIcs **40**, pp.915–930. Schloss Dagstuhl–Leibniz-Zentrum für Informatik, 2015. DOI [10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2015.915](https://doi.org/10.4230/LIPIcs.APPROX-RANDOM.2015.915). Published 13 August 2015. Original proceedings full text inspected. Proposition 13, pp.923–924, contains precisely the eight certificates and D(4-MAJ)=4; preceding paragraph attributes the function to Savický. Proposition 14 concerns scalar zero-error randomized query complexity and does not address exact certificate-output quantum complexity. Preprint arXiv:1504.01339; no later journal version located in this bounded review.

2. **Petr Savický.** “On determinism versus unambiguous nondeterminism for decision trees.” *Electronic Colloquium on Computational Complexity*, TR02-009, 2002, 6pp. [Official record](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2002/009/), [original PDF](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/report/2002/009/download/). Record dated 17 January 2002; publication timestamp 21 January 2002. The record misspells “unambiquous”; use the standard spelling in the bibliography while retaining the official identifier. The author publication list identifies it as an ECCC report; no journal version located. Scope: deterministic decision-tree **size**, unambiguous partitions, and the earlier scalar construction. Original PDF captured; substantive priority concerning the function is supported here through KRS's explicit attribution, not a claimed fresh full proof audit of Savický.

3. **Howard Barnum, Michael Saks, and Mario Szegedy.** “Quantum query complexity and semi-definite programming.” In *Proceedings of the 18th Annual IEEE Conference on Computational Complexity (CCC 2003)*, pp.179–193. IEEE Computer Society, 2003. DOI [10.1109/CCC.2003.1214419](https://doi.org/10.1109/CCC.2003.1214419). The original-authored proceedings text was consulted via its available author-uploaded copy; its general query/output SDP separates query evolution from output conditions, including arbitrary finite output alphabets in the model. Theorem 5 (§9.3) specializes a zero-error feasibility statement to partial Boolean functions. This is a predecessor for exact SDP/Gram feasibility and rules out presenting the method as new. No occurrence of this specific eight-output partition was located. The earlier captured technical version is **“Quantum Decision Trees and Semidefinite Programming,” Los Alamos report LA-UR-01-6417**, with title page dated **23 May 2002**, not the proceedings title/year; [original technical PDF](https://www.osti.gov/servlets/purl/975874/). Its cover and title/authors were verified; glyph-encoded body was not used as the sole source for a fine-grained theorem citation. No journal version located. The 2003 attribution is also independently given by MJM Theorem 4 and LMRSS reference BSS03.

4. **Ashley Montanaro, Richard Jozsa, and Graeme Mitchison.** “On exact quantum query complexity.” *Algorithmica* **71**(4), pp.775–796, 2015. DOI [10.1007/s00453-013-9826-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00453-013-9826-8). [arXiv:1111.0475v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.0475v2), revised 9 September 2013, 28pp; arXiv describes v2 as essentially the published version. V1 is 2 November 2011, so do not substitute the older author PDF silently. Inspected v2: Definition 1/Theorem 4, p.6, give the BSS SDP characterization for Boolean-output functions, and §4.1 pp.7 onward reconstructs algorithms from feasible Grams. §5 p.11 and Appendix A contain numerical studies of Boolean functions on at most four bits (and symmetric Boolean functions on six); §5 explicitly warns that numerical feasibility does not prove exact query complexity. These tables are not an eight-output certificate classification. Even a scalar 4-MAJ algorithm only returns its Boolean value and need not identify the containing edge.

5. **Troy Lee, Rajat Mittal, Ben W. Reichardt, Robert Špalek, and Mario Szegedy.** “Quantum Query Complexity of State Conversion.” In *2011 IEEE 52nd Annual Symposium on Foundations of Computer Science (FOCS 2011)*, pp.344–353. IEEE Computer Society, 2011. DOI [10.1109/FOCS.2011.75](https://doi.org/10.1109/FOCS.2011.75). Author full version [arXiv:1011.3020v2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.3020v2), 23 August 2011, 19pp; [author PDF](https://www.ucw.cz/~robert/papers/state-conversion.pdf) captured and inspected. Theorem 1.1 p.2 is a bounded-error constant-factor characterization for arbitrary finite input/output alphabets. Definitions 2.1–2.2 p.3 distinguish coherent/noncoherent output conditions. Lemma 4.7 p.8 gives the exact-conversion query-distance lower bound (one query changes the filtered norm by at most two). Theorem 4.9 pp.9–10 is an error-dependent approximate characterization, not an exact-integer query classification. No evaluation yielding the specific four-query certificate claim was located. No journal version located in this bounded review.

6. **Yuval Filmus, Edward A. Hirsch, Sascha Kurz, Ferdinand Ihringer, Artur Riazanov, Alexander V. Smal, and Marc Vinyals.** “Irreducible Subcube Partitions.” *Electronic Journal of Combinatorics* **30**(3), P3.29, 2023, 51pp. DOI [10.37236/11862](https://doi.org/10.37236/11862). [Journal full text](https://www.combinatorics.org/ojs/index.php/eljc/article/download/v30i3p29/pdf/); arXiv:2212.14685 has v3 (18 August 2023, 39pp) and a materially revised v4 (8 September 2026, 37pp). The theorem locators 2.49 and 2.54 above refer to **current v4**, not automatically the journal pagination. The final and both author versions were captured in the earlier audit. No quantum evaluation of this certificate problem was found in the inspected catalogue text.

## Search record and logical exclusions

Searches combined `4-MAJ`, `quaternary majority`, `subcube partition`, `irreducible subcube`, `perfect matching`, `exact quantum query`, `certificate output`, `state conversion`, and `semidefinite programming`, and followed original references from KRS, the irreducible-partition catalogue, MJM, and LMRSS. Searches also checked publication/author records for later journal versions. These were bounded searches, not an exhaustive search of all citing papers or unpublished computations.

The strongest exact prior hit is KRS Proposition 13, with a direct certificate-by-certificate match. BSS supplies general methodology; MJM supplies Boolean numerical classifications and an explicit warning about numerical exactness; LMRSS supplies general state-conversion bounds but no automatic exact-three-versus-four implication. A future discovery of an exact published evaluation, dual certificate, or software catalogue for this finite partition would change the priority status without changing the fresh proof audit.
